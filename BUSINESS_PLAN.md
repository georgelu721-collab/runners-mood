# Runner's Mood — Business Plan

**Tagline:** Meet them at mile one.
**One-liner:** Runner's Mood matches you with people you have actually run with. After a group run, you choose your mood (a running buddy, a crew, or a date), and you only connect when the feeling is mutual.

> Status: concept / pre-seed. Every figure in the financial section is a planning assumption. Market statistics should be re-verified against current sources before they go in a pitch deck.

---

## 1. Executive summary

People are lonelier than ever, dating apps are losing users to swipe fatigue, and run clubs have become the place where people in their 20s and 30s go to meet each other. The run club scene has a social problem it can't solve on its own: you run next to someone for 5 km, have a great chat, and then they disappear into the crowd at the coffee shop. There is no low-pressure way to follow up.

Runner's Mood fixes the follow-up. Runners check in at a club run with a QR code. For 24 hours after the run (the **Cooldown**), they can privately send a signal to anyone who was there:

| Mood | Signal | Meaning |
|---|---|---|
| **Pace** | a wave | "Want to run together again?" (platonic, 1:1) |
| **Crew** | a high-five | "Join our group / grab coffee after runs" (platonic, group) |
| **Spark** | a spark | "I'd like to take you on a date" (romantic) |

Signals are invisible unless they're returned. If both people send something, they match, and the match **settles at the gentler of the two moods**. If I send a Spark and you send a Wave, we become running buddies and nobody is embarrassed. That single rule removes the main fear that stops people from making a move in real life.

We make money from a consumer subscription (**Mood+**), paid tools for run clubs (**Club Pro**), brand-sponsored runs, and ticketed events. We launch city by city through run club captains, who get free tools that make their clubs easier to run.

**The ask:** $2.5M pre-seed to launch in 3 cities, reach 150 partner clubs and 60,000 registered runners in 18 months.

## 2. The problem

1. **The anti-social era.** In 2023 the U.S. Surgeon General issued an advisory naming loneliness and isolation a public health epidemic. Young adults report the fewest close friendships of any generation surveyed, and making friends after college is hard because there are few repeated, low-stakes places to meet the same people.
2. **Dating app fatigue.** The major dating apps have seen paying-user declines as users complain about endless swiping, ghosting, catfishing, and safety. Gen Z in particular says they want to meet people "in real life."
3. **Run clubs became the new bar, but they lack a follow-up tool.** Social run clubs have grown fast and are now widely described as a dating and friendship scene. But the connection breaks at the end of the run: asking for someone's number in front of 40 people is awkward, and Instagram follows are vague. Club organisers also juggle WhatsApp groups, Instagram DMs, spreadsheets and Strava to run their clubs.

## 3. The solution

A mobile app (iOS + Android) with two sides:

### For runners
- **Check in** at a run by scanning the club's QR code at the start line (or by GPS geofence + start time). This creates a verified "shared run."
- **Run card.** After the run you get a card: route, distance, pace groups, and the people who were there (only people who checked in, and only those who opted into being visible).
- **Cooldown window (24h).** Send a Wave, High-five or Spark to anyone from the run. Nobody sees signals that aren't returned.
- **Gentle-mood matching.** Mutual signals become a match at the gentler mood (Spark + Wave = Pace match).
- **Plan the next run, not a date at a bar.** Matches get a shared calendar of upcoming club runs, so the next meeting happens in a public, familiar, group setting.
- **Pace profile.** Typical pace (min/km or min/mi), favourite distances, run times (dawn, lunch, evening), and goals (first 10K, marathon block). Used to suggest compatible running buddies.

### For run clubs
- Free event pages, QR check-in, attendance history, and a member list.
- Waivers, capacity limits, pace group sign-ups.
- **Club Pro:** paid ticketing, analytics (new vs returning runners, retention), sponsor matching, and merch drops.

### What we deliberately don't build
- **No browsing a profile feed of strangers.** You can only signal people you've physically run with. This is the core safety and authenticity guarantee.
- **No open DMs before a match.**
- **No public "who liked you" counts.** The point is to lower the stakes, not gamify rejection.

## 4. Why now

- Run clubs are a cultural moment: social clubs focused on community over speed, brand-hosted runs, and "run club is the new dating app" coverage across mainstream media.
- Dating apps are cutting costs and losing paying users; investors and users are looking for in-person-first alternatives.
- Loneliness is a mainstream public health conversation, and "third places" are scarce.
- Fitness data is already social (Strava, Garmin), so runners are comfortable sharing routes and paces.

## 7. Competition

| Competitor | What they do | Gap Runner's Mood fills |
|---|---|---|
| Hinge, Bumble, Tinder | Swipe-based dating with strangers | No shared real-world context; safety and authenticity concerns |
| Bumble For Friends, Timeleft, Meetup | Friend-making, dinners with strangers, event listings | Not built around a repeated weekly activity; no mutual-signal system |
| Strava (clubs, kudos) | Activity tracking, social feed | Not designed for meeting people; signalling interest is public and awkward |
| WhatsApp / Instagram | What clubs use today | Chaotic, no check-in, no privacy around interest |
| Niche running dating apps | Profile feeds filtered by "runner" | Still swiping strangers; no proof you've met |

**Our moat**
1. **Verified co-presence.** Every connection starts with a shared run. This is hard to fake and is the core trust signal.
2. **Club network effects.** Clubs adopt free tools → their runners join → more runners make clubs pick us. Each city becomes harder to enter for a competitor.
3. **Gentle-mood matching** is a brand-defining mechanic that makes people comfortable trying.
4. **Data:** attendance, pace, and match outcomes let us recommend the right runs for each person.

## 8. Business model

| Stream | Price | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| **Mood+ subscription** | $9.99/mo or $59.99/yr | Extended Cooldown (72h), see past 10 runs' attendees, pace-buddy suggestions across clubs, travel mode (check into runs in other cities), priority to sold-out runs |
| **Club Pro** | $49/mo per club | Paid ticketing, analytics, waivers, custom branding. Free tier always available |
| **Ticketing fee** | 5% + $0.50 per paid ticket | Special events: themed runs, race-day meetups, singles runs, run + brunch |
| **Brand partnerships** | $2K–$25K per activation | Sponsored runs, gear test runs, post-run pop-ups; we route a share to the hosting club |
| **Runner's Mood events** | $15–$45 per ticket | Flagship "Spark Runs" (singles), "Crew Socials", and city race-weekend shakeouts |

**Revenue share principle:** clubs earn a share of sponsorship and ticket revenue from their events. Captains are our distribution, so they should make money with us.

## 9. Go-to-market

### Phase 0 — Pre-launch (months 0–3)
- Waitlist via the concept site; target runners in the first launch city.
- Recruit 30 **Founding Clubs** with free Club Pro for life and a revenue share.
- Recruit 50 **Pacers** (ambassadors): club regulars who host the QR check-in and welcome new runners.

### Phase 1 — First city (months 3–9)
- Launch in one dense, run-club-heavy city (shortlist: New York, London, Los Angeles; final pick depends on founding club commitments).
- Weekly Runner's Mood-branded "Spark Run" and "Crew Run" in partnership with clubs.
- Content: "Cooldown Stories" (couples and friend groups who met on a run), shot for TikTok/Instagram Reels.
- Target: 50 clubs, 15K registered users, 40% of check-ins sending at least one signal.

### Phase 2 — Expand (months 9–18)
- Add 2 more cities with the same playbook.
- Brand partnership program with running shoe and apparel companies.
- Race-weekend activations at major marathons (shakeout runs open to visitors with travel mode).

### Phase 3 — Scale (year 2–3)
- 25 cities across the US, UK, Europe and Australia.
- Open Club Pro self-serve; partner API with Strava and Garmin for automatic check-in.

### Key channels
1. Run club captains (primary).
2. Social video: real match stories, "mood reveal" moments.
3. Brand co-marketing with running retailers.
4. Referral: "Bring a buddy" gives both runners 1 free month of Mood+.

## 12. Trust & safety

Safety is the product, not a feature.

- **Matches only from shared runs**, verified by check-in. No cold contact from strangers.
- **Optional ID + photo verification** (required for Spark mode).
- **Signals stay private** unless mutual. Mood is never displayed to the group.
- **Gentle-mood matching** removes the risk of awkward romantic rejection.
- **Next meeting defaults to a group run** in a public place.
- **Club-level moderation:** captains and Pacers can report and remove members; reports across clubs are shared within Runner's Mood.
- **Block = invisible.** Blocked users never appear on your run cards again.
- **Location privacy:** routes hide the first and last 400 m by default so home addresses aren't exposed.
- **Code of Conduct** that clubs agree to, with a clear "no means run on" culture.

## 14. Risks and mitigations

| Risk | Mitigation |
|---|---|
| Clubs push back on "dating app" vibe | Platonic-first branding; Pace and Crew are the default moods; clubs can disable Spark for their runs |
| Harassment or unwanted attention | Co-presence requirement, private signals, verification, fast moderation, captain controls |
| Cold-start in a new city | Launch only after 20+ clubs commit; run our own flagship events |
| Seasonality (winter) | Indoor track sessions, treadmill-and-coffee events, Southern Hemisphere cities |
| Strava or dating apps copy the feature | Club network and brand trust; move fast city by city |
| Low paid conversion | Shift weight to club, ticketing, and brand revenue, which don't depend on consumer subscriptions |
