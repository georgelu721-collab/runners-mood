How it works

Four splits from
stranger to friend.

Run clubs already bring people together every week. Runner's Mood handles the awkward part after the run.

Split 10.0 km

Check in

Scan your club's QR code at the start line. It proves you were there and puts you on the run card.

Split 26.4 km

Run

Run like you always do. Chat in your pace group. The app stays in your pocket.

Split 3+24 h

Cooldown

For 24 hours, privately send a Wave, a High-five or a Spark to anyone who ran with you.

Split 4Next run

Match

If they sent one back, you match. Pick a club run to do together next week.

Pick your mood

A running buddy

Find someone who holds your pace and shows up at 6 a.m. Pace matches compare your usual min/km, distances and run times.

  • Signal: a Wave
  • Best for: long-run partners, race training, accountability
  • Suggested next step: the same club run next week

The gentle-mood rule

The match settles on the gentler mood.

If you send a Spark and they send a Wave, you become running buddies. They never learn you sent a Spark. Nobody gets rejected in front of the club.

Trust & safety

Built for people
you've met in daylight.

Shared runs only

You can only signal people who checked in to the same run. No feed of strangers, no cold messages.

Private signals

Nobody sees a signal unless it's returned. Your mood is never shown to the group.

Verified for Spark

Photo and ID verification is required before you can send or receive a Spark.

Group-first plans

Your next meetup defaults to a club run in a public place, with people around.

Captain controls

Club captains can remove members, and clubs can switch Spark off for their runs.

Route privacy

The first and last 400 m of every route are hidden, so your home stays private.

Why now

The run club is the
new town square.

Loneliness is a health issue

In 2023 the U.S. Surgeon General called loneliness and isolation an epidemic. People want somewhere to see the same faces every week.

Swipe fatigue is real

Dating apps are losing paying users. People say they want to meet someone in real life, with real context.

Run clubs are booming

Social run clubs fill up every week in cities worldwide. The missing piece is a low-pressure way to follow up.
